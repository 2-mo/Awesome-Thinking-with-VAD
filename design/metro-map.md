# Research publication network

The diagram has three explicit dimensions: publication year on the horizontal axis, displayed publication venue on the vertical axis, and five colored method-school routes through the papers. The catalog determines the station count, year zones and displayed venue categories. Conference rows descend by full-catalog paper count; equal counts retain the established venue order, and unlisted venues break ties alphabetically. Filtering preserves that order. WACV and workshop papers are excluded from the curated paper catalog. NeurIPS and NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks share one NeurIPS row and publication-filter category; exact track metadata is preserved in the catalog and paper details. arXiv retains its own row at the bottom, outside the conference ranking.

Stations use compact paper names; the axes supply venue and year, and the interactive title and detail panel preserve full publication metadata. Route colors identify editorial method families. Crossings without a station do not represent method fusion, citation or inheritance. There are no invented interchange stations. Within each year, verified months determine the left-to-right order across all venue rows. Months remain hidden; from 2025 onward, Q1–Q4 labels provide a second tier beneath each year. Quarter dividers follow the packed month groups, empty quarters retain narrow muted slots, and unknown months remain in a separate undated slot. Quarter widths are schematic rather than equal calendar intervals. Conference papers use the main-conference month; arXiv papers use their first-submission month. The `timeline` metadata records the basis and primary source for each paper. This is a schematic sequence, not a proportional calendar or a claim about acceptance/publication dates; same-month ties are spaced for legibility. Papers without verified month metadata sort after known months in that year.

`src/components/publication-layout.ts` separates layout from scientific data. It packs full-catalog stations into hidden month groups inside year/venue bands, allocates distinct method baselines in dense cells, and places labels above or below according to the incoming/outgoing route. Combined benchmark/model titles use the shorter model name on the map. Every route candidate and visibility-search edge must move rightwards, so label avoidance cannot introduce backward loops. Routing uses horizontal, vertical and 45-degree segments while avoiding label boxes and unrelated paper nodes. The renderer rounds bends and uses a narrow paper-colored casing to make crossings legible. Hover or keyboard focus emphasizes the corresponding method route. Search preserves the full-catalog geometry.

Visual vocabulary was informed by the [official Transport for London Tube map](https://tfl.gov.uk/cdn/static/cms/documents/standard-tube-map.pdf): colored lines, station markers and separate labels. No TfL artwork, fonts, marks or map geometry is copied. The earlier free-position innovation routes and generated panorama are superseded by this coordinate-based network; decorative source images remain historical design assets.

The renderer uses broader rounded bends, including compact curves beneath station markers. Near labels, corner rounding shrinks to preserve text clearance. Paper stations use larger colored rings, center dots and opaque paper-colored backplates; hover, keyboard focus and selection strengthen the marker. Smoothing preserves the station coordinates and forward-only route skeleton computed by the layout.

All drawing and text is vector-based and included in exported SVGs. The overview stays on one screen with pan/zoom and an on-demand detail overlay.
